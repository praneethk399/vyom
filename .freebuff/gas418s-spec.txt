                                      KEY FEATURES

· Two engine versions are available to meet the widest range of installation
    requirements. Both horizontal and vertical axis installation are possible.

· The GAS418S has a very compact size. It is thus possible to fit it into very small
    fuselages, helping to keep the overall weight low. Specific power is very high, going
    up to 2.31 hp/kg in the horizontal version. Peak power at sea level is 180hp
    @5800RPM.

· The GAS418S relies on a single stage centrifugal supercharger to increase its
    performance at high altitude. This supercharger is driven mechanically rather than
    by an exhaust gas turbine.

           o The exhaust gases can thus exit the engine effortlessly, reducing exhaust and
               combustion chamber temperatures with considerable benefits on the life of
               the engine.

           o Medium-high altitude performance of the mechanical supercharger are better
               than the turbocharger ones. The lower air density modifies the expansion
               ratio of the turbine, limiting the overall boosting capability due to
               anticipated turbine over speed.

· Fuel injection and ignition are completely electronic and ECU controlled, making the
    engine run at its best under a wide range of ambient conditions.

· All employed parts are manufactured in Italy, or in countries of the European Union.
    The engine is completely designed and assembled in house at Flygas, based in Emilia
    Romagna, Italy, (the world "Motor Valley").

· GAS418S engine runs with the standard 98 octane number automotive gasoline.

                         ENGINE MANAGEMENT

                                                                 Figure 7 - ECU wiring

The engine is controlled by an EFI Technology Euro 2 ECU. The ECU input signals are:
             · Crankshaft rotational speed and angular position
             · Intake manifold air pressure (MAP)
             · Lambda probe signal
             · Intake manifold air temperature
             · Throttle position
             · Coil test switch signal

Consequently, a suitable set of engine sensors is needed. A simplified connection scheme
is shown in Figure 8.

ECU communication protocol is CAN bus 2.0B.
